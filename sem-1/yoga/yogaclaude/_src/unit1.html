UNIT I

Foundation of Yoga

3 hours

1. Meaning, Definition & Objectives

In one line: Yoga comes from the Sanskrit root Yuj = “to join / unite / yoke” — joining body, mind, breath and awareness.
  • Holistic: it looks at the whole person, not just exercise.
  • General definition: a system of postures, breathing, concentration, meditation and relaxation for physical and mental well-being.
  • Patanjali: “Yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ” — controlling the ups and downs (fluctuations) of the mind.
  • Practical definition: a way to build health, calmness, self-awareness, discipline and a balanced life.

9 objectives: (1) physical health & fitness (2) flexibility, balance, coordination, body awareness (3) comfortable, controlled breathing (4) concentration (5) manage everyday stress (6) relaxation & emotional awareness (7) patience, discipline, self-control (8) healthy daily habits (9) self-awareness & a balanced approach to life.

2. Origin & Development

Yoga began in ancient India and grew slowly over centuries; its exact origin cannot be fixed with certainty.

3. Importance in Modern Life

Modern life = long sitting, screens, irregular routines, pressure. Yoga adds movement, breath awareness and relaxation.

  • Physical: flexibility, balance, posture, mobility, endurance; awareness of limits.
  • Mental: calmer mind, awareness of thoughts and emotions.
  • Stress: a pause to release tension and shift attention.
  • Concentration: practise returning attention when distracted.
  • Healthy habits: regular movement, self-discipline; works with sleep and nutrition.
  • Education: posture awareness while studying, pre-exam relaxation, patience, regular habits.
Limit: yoga is not a substitute for medical treatment or all other exercise — use it inside a balanced lifestyle.

5. Yoga & Mental Well-Being

Mental well-being = managing daily demands, understanding emotions, keeping relationships, deciding well. Yoga mixes movement, breath, attention and relaxation.

  • Less mental tension: stress shows as tight muscles, fast breathing, repeating thoughts — gentle yoga may settle it.
  • Emotional awareness: notice emotions without reacting at once (e.g. exam nervousness → brief relaxation).
  • Self-awareness: observe sensations, breath, attention, comfort.
  • Mood: some feel better; may help some anxiety/depression symptoms but results vary — not a replacement for professional care.
  • Patience & self-control: steady practice beats competing or forcing poses.
  • Students: routines of movement, relaxation, awareness; plus sleep, activity, social support, help when needed.

6. Yoga & Stress Management

Stress = body-and-mind response to demands or perceived threats. Some stress motivates; too much or too long disturbs sleep, concentration, mood. Student sources: exams, assignments, deadlines, family expectations, social pressure, uncertain future.

6 ways yoga helps: ① breathing awareness (less rapid breathing) ② muscle relaxation ③ attention regulation (away from worries) ④ body awareness (raised shoulders, tense jaw) ⑤ regular routine = self-care time ⑥ relaxation/rest after effort.

PracticeHowTime
Breathing awarenessSit comfortably, watch natural breath, don't force2–5 min
Balasana (modified)Rest kneeling with support; choose another rest pose if kneeling is hard20–60 sec

5-minute routine: 1 min feel body sensations → 2 min watch natural breathing → 1 min relax shoulders, jaw, hands, face → 1 min notice surroundings, return to present.

Avoid forced deep breathing, long breath-holding or rapid breathing without guidance. Dizziness/breathlessness/distress → stop, breathe normally.

7. Yoga & Concentration

Concentration = directing attention to one task/object/thought while limiting distractions (needed for reading, listening, problem-solving). In yoga you focus on alignment and balance in an asana, or on the breath; when the mind wanders you gently bring it back — that repetition is the training.

Helpful practices: breath awareness, single-pointed attention, basic meditation, balance postures, body awareness, mindful movement with breath.

Breath counting (2–3 min): sit, spine naturally upright → shoulders and face relaxed → breathe naturally → count each full breath 1 to 5, then restart at 1 → if distracted, return to 1 without self-criticism.
Outcome: practise directing attention, noticing distraction, returning focus. Also helped by sleep, exercise, less screen time.

8. Yoga & Flexibility

Flexibility = ability of a joint (or joints) to move through an appropriate range. Depends on joint structure, tissue properties, age, activity level, past injuries. It supports bending, reaching, turning, sitting, walking — and is not the same as doing extreme poses. Yoga uses controlled postures that slowly take muscles through a comfortable range; improvement varies by person.

Examples: Tadasana (alignment, balance) · seated side stretch · Cat–Cow (gentle spine movement, hands and knees) · standing side bend.

7 principles: warm up first · move slowly, no jerks · stretch to a mild sensation only · breathe normally · never force a joint · practise regularly, not extremely once · modify for age, ability, health.

Stop on sharp pain, tingling, numbness or joint pain — these are not good stretch feelings.

10. Basic Principles of Yoga Practice

  1. Individual ability — adapt poses to you, not to others.
  2. Comfortable alignment — spine in natural position, no forced extreme joint ranges.
  3. Movement + breath — usually inhale while lengthening/opening, exhale while folding/relaxing (a general convention, not a rigid rule).
  4. No unnecessary force — no competitive stretching or jerks; progress gradually.
  5. Awareness — alignment, breath, tension, discomfort signs.
  6. Relaxed mind — aim to learn, be comfortable, move in control, not look perfect.
  7. Progress gradually — simple poses, short holds first.
  8. Systematic sequence — preparation → warm-up → main practice → breathing → relaxation.
  9. Suitable place — clean, ventilated, well-lit, obstacle-free, non-slip mat.
  10. Qualified instructor — correct alignment, modifications, safety; medical cases need individual advice.

11. Regularity & Discipline

Regularity = practising consistently over time. Discipline = following a suitable routine, respecting safety rules, practising with patience and commitment. Occasional practice gives temporary relaxation; lasting gains in flexibility, balance, strength need consistency.

Discipline gives: consistency & patience · attention to technique · following a plan · respect for limits · responsibility for health · no rushing into advanced practice.

Beginner routine: ~10–15 min sessions, increase slowly. A week = gentle yoga, joint mobility, breathing awareness, suitable asanas, relaxation, plus rest days/lighter sessions. Quality over quantity — a few poses done well beat many done wrong; never raise time/intensity at the cost of comfort or safety.

12. Role of Breathing

Breathing awareness = noticing natural inhalation and exhalation without trying to change it (watch abdomen, ribs, chest). Phases: inhalation (air in) · exhalation (air out) · natural pause. Beginners should not deliberately lengthen pauses or hold the breath.

Why it matters: ① supports relaxation ② improves awareness ③ coordinates movement ④ supports concentration ⑤ shows tension/effort/discomfort.

Abdominal breathing awareness (2–3 min): lie on back, knees bent, feet on floor (or sit supported) → one hand on abdomen → relax shoulders and face → breathe naturally, feel abdomen move → don't push it out or force the breath → return attention to surroundings before getting up.
Benefits: breathing awareness, a focus for attention, may support relaxation.

Basic yogic breathing types: natural breath awareness · diaphragmatic breathing · Nadi Shodhana (alternate-nostril) · Bhramari (gentle humming on exhale). Beginners start with natural and comfortable diaphragmatic breathing; specialised techniques only with supervision.

Precautions: never force breath · avoid long retention · no rapid/very deep breathing to dizziness · stop for dizziness, chest discomfort, unusual breathlessness, distress · medical conditions → get professional guidance first.

13. Warm-Up & Preparation

Warm-up = gentle activity before the main session. 6 objectives: gradually increase movement & muscle activity · mobilise joints comfortably · prepare muscles · improve alignment awareness · find stiffness/discomfort early · set a calm, attentive approach.

Components: general preparation (stand/sit, natural breath) · joint mobility (neck, shoulders, wrists, hips, knees, ankles) · dynamic movements (shoulder rolls, arm raises, gentle spinal movement) · breathing awareness.

5-minute warm-up (illustrative)

14. General Precautions

Yoga suits most people when adapted, but it is not risk-free; safety depends on health, practice type, instructor, and pose suitability.

WhenDo
BeforeClean, stable, non-slip surface · comfortable clothes · clear objects away · warm up · tell instructor about injuries/limits/health concerns · don't start a hard session when acutely unwell
DuringMove slowly with control · no jerks or over-stretching · don't compete · breathe comfortably, no needless breath-holding · use chair, wall, cushion, block · stop for sharp pain, numbness, tingling, unusual discomfort · don't continue just because others can
AfterRelax · rise gradually from lying · notice how you feel · drink water · avoid rushing into demanding activity if light-headed

Need modified practice and professional guidance: recent surgery, acute injuries, severe balance problems, certain heart conditions, uncontrolled blood pressure, glaucoma, pregnancy concerns. Not every posture suits every person.

Stop if: sharp/worsening pain · dizziness or faintness · chest pain or pressure · unusual/severe shortness of breath · new numbness or tingling · sudden weakness or loss of balance. Chest pain, fainting or severe breathing trouble = urgent medical attention.

15. Contraindications

Contraindication = a condition where a posture/technique should be avoided because it may be risky. It may apply to one practice, not to all yoga (e.g. cannot kneel → still do seated or standing work).

ConditionPrecaution / modification
Neck pain/injuryAvoid forceful neck movements and postures that load the unsupported neck
Lower-back pain/injuryAvoid painful deep forward bends, forceful twists, movements that aggravate symptoms
Knee pain/injuryAvoid painful kneeling or deep knee bends; use supports or alternatives
Wrist painModify weight-bearing poses or use a non-weight-bearing alternative
High blood pressureAvoid demanding inversions or strenuous breath-holding unless medically cleared and supervised
Glaucoma / certain eye conditionsSeek specialist advice on head-down/inversion postures
PregnancyPregnancy-appropriate modifications; guidance from qualified instructor and healthcare professional
Recent surgeryMedical clearance before resuming physical practice
Dizziness / balance problemsSupported postures; avoid unsupported balance challenges
Breathing / cardiovascular disordersSuitable intensity and breathing practices, with professional guidance

These are general examples, not a medical assessment — diagnosis, severity, recovery stage and the doctor's advice all matter.

Contraindication vs modification: contraindication = don't do that posture/technique in those circumstances; modification = adapt it to be easier or more suitable (hold a wall/chair for balance; seated option for knee pain). Individualisation: adapt yoga to the person, not the person to the pose. Teachers give alternatives, respect feedback, and don't diagnose or treat beyond their competence.

16. Relaxation After Practice

Relaxation = reducing unnecessary muscle tension and letting body and mind rest (lying, sitting, watching breath, or relaxing body parts one by one).

Why: ① reduces leftover tension (shoulders, jaw, hands) ② encourages calmness ③ develops body awareness ④ gives a gradual end to the session ⑤ practises mindful attention to the present.

Shavasana (Corpse Pose) — about 3–5 min or more: lie on back → legs rest apart naturally → arms beside body, palms up (or comfortable) → cushion for head/knees if needed → eyes closed or softly open → watch natural breath → relax face, shoulders, arms, abdomen, legs, feet → stay still → deepen awareness of surroundings before moving fingers and toes → roll to one side, sit up slowly.

Alternatives (back pain, pregnancy, breathing difficulty, discomfort): supported side-lying, reclined, or chair. Nobody should be forced to close eyes, lie a certain way, or stay still if it distresses them.

PART B — PRACTICAL COMPONENT Focus: correct technique, comfort, breath, alignment, safety — not speed or performance.

18. Joint Mobility Exercises

Joint mobility = moving a joint through its available range in a controlled, comfortable way (neck, shoulders, wrists, spine, hips, knees, ankles). Objectives: keep/improve comfortable movement · movement control · prepare for postures · notice limits and asymmetries · learn safe patterns · regular movement for people who sit long.

A. NeckSit upright, shoulders relaxed → turn head right, centre, left → tilt slightly to each shoulder3–5 each directionNo forcing, no pulling head with hands, never into pain

Assessment: identify the joint · correct starting position · perform safely · appropriate breathing · follow directions · know when to modify or stop.

19. Basic Breathing Awareness

Observing natural breathing without forcing, speeding up or controlling it — the simplest link between breath, attention and relaxation. Objectives: awareness of inhalation/exhalation · comfortable breathing · attention to sensations · a basic way to settle attention · preparation for structured breathing later.

Seated breathing awareness (2–3 min): sit cross-legged on mat, on a cushion, or on a chair with feet supported → spine naturally upright → hands on thighs → relax shoulders, jaw, face → eyes closed or softly open → watch breath enter and leave → notice abdomen/chest/ribs moving → don't take deliberately deeper breaths → if mind wanders, return to the breath → at the end notice surroundings and resume activity gradually.

Learning outcomes: explain breathing awareness · identify inhalation/exhalation · keep a comfortable sitting posture · observe natural breath without forcing · see why attention and relaxation matter · recognise signs to stop.

Teacher: speak slowly, simple instructions; no identical breathing pattern for all (it varies). If a student has dizziness, breathlessness, panic or discomfort → stop focusing on the breath, breathe normally, rest.

20. Correct Sitting & Standing Posture

Posture = how the body is positioned when sitting, standing, moving. Aim: stable, comfortable alignment without strain. No single rigid posture suits everyone — the goal is comfortable alignment, not a perfectly straight body at all times.

Sitting (mat, cushion or chair)
  1. Legs comfortable if cross-legged; don't force knees to floor
  2. Pelvis stable
  3. Spine keeps natural curves
  4. Head balanced over shoulders
  5. Shoulders relaxed, not pulled back
  6. Hands on thighs or knees
  7. Jaw and face relaxed
  8. Breathe naturally
Benefits: spinal-alignment awareness; stable base for breathing/meditation; less needless tension; finds a comfortable position for study. Mistakes: slumping · raised shoulders · exaggerated back arch · rigid body · cross-legged despite knee/hip pain. Modification: chair, cushion or other supported position.
Standing (feet hip-width or comfortable)
  1. Weight spread over both feet
  2. Knees relaxed, not locked
  3. Pelvis and spine natural
  4. Chest open without pushing forward
  5. Shoulders rest naturally
  6. Head balanced over trunk
  7. Look forward at a comfortable level
  8. Breathe normally, stay relaxed
This is the basic alignment of Tadasana (Mountain Pose).
Common standing errorCorrection
Leaning too far forward/backwardReturn to a comfortable, balanced position
Knees locked rigidlyLet knees stay relaxed
Shoulders raised to earsLet shoulders settle naturally
Weight mostly on one footRedistribute weight comfortably
Head pushed forwardBring head to a more balanced position
Excessive lower-back archLet spine keep its natural curves

Classroom activity: stand comfortably and observe feet, knees, pelvis, shoulders, head → notice weight distribution → relax shoulders and jaw → adjust until stable → take 3 natural breaths → compare before and after. Aim is awareness, not judging bodies or demanding identical postures.

21. Body Awareness Exercises

Body awareness = noticing position, movement, tension and sensation of body parts. It shows how you move, where you hold tension, and whether a pose feels comfortable or strained. Importance: recognise position/alignment · controlled movement · spot needless tension · know your limits · supports balance and coordination · know when to rest or modify.
